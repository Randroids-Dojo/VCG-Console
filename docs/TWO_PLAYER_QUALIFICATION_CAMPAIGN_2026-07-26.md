# Two-player qualification campaign

Date: 2026-07-26
Scope: I-054
Status: blocked strict zero-result pre-registration

## Claim boundary

This campaign defines the evidence required after the complete one-player
milestone passes. It does not claim that a target, tracker, room, camera,
participant pair, calibration path, accessible outline, runtime freeze
adapter, two-player action path, or physical trial exists or passes.

The current state-machine and appearance-free identity fixtures are useful
desk evidence. They cannot qualify real crossing, occlusion, assignment,
comprehension, action ownership, room safety, latency, recovery, or gameplay.
No two-player collection begins until the exact I-053 result and every
required one-player cell pass. Four-player work remains gated on I-054.

## Frozen product semantics

The campaign preserves D-071 through D-076 and D-135:

- Player 1 joins and calibrates before Player 2.
- Tracker identifiers are opaque session-local continuity evidence, not
  people, profiles, credentials, or automatic body/face matches.
- Each player receives a number and a pattern/shape plus color; color alone
  is never the distinguishing cue.
- Any joined player may pause. The earliest completed long-X owns the
  overlay; equal-update completions go to the lower player slot.
- Only the visible launcher or overlay owner moves shared focus.
- Confirmed loss of either player freezes the whole game: simulation,
  timers, hazards, scoring, and actions do not advance.
- Silent recovery requires every unsafe original track. A wrong track cannot
  substitute, and post-timeout recovery requires deliberate Resume or an
  explicit non-empty roster reduction.
- Explicit leave removes authority immediately; a later re-entry is a fresh
  join, never an implicit restoration.

## Prerequisite gate

Before any operation, bind a complete one-player result proving the exact
selected target, build, room and safe play zone, camera/capture policy,
calibration, tracker configuration, blocking personas, placements, action
gates, negative sessions, exposure clock, and data policy. Every required
one-player cell must pass. Synthetic evidence, an aggregate, a different
target, or a best-case placement cannot replace this result.

The two-player campaign then separately binds:

- the exact selected prototype target and clean runtime tuple;
- one selected two-player tracker configuration;
- camera, capture, calibration, and timestamp protocols;
- measured two-player subzones inside the qualified room;
- ordered participant-pair cohort and consent/assent protocols;
- independent identity, action, menu-owner, and freeze-state oracles;
- the accessible outline and instruction build;
- shell and obstacle-sample freeze/state-integrity adapters;
- resource telemetry and a common clock;
- minimized data handling, deletion, schedule, and operator protocols.

All bindings and operation authorities remain null or false in the tracked
plan.

## Required matrices

### Ordered player-pair classes

Order matters because Player 1 receives initial launcher ownership and wins
equal-update pause ties. Run all four classes independently:

1. school-age child as P1 with school-age child as P2;
2. school-age child as P1 with adult as P2;
3. adult as P1 with school-age child as P2; and
4. adult as P1 with adult as P2.

The minimum number of independent participant pairs per class remains an
owner gate. One pair, one class, or reversing labels after collection cannot
rescue another class.

### Paired room placements

Run side-by-side center, side-by-side left, side-by-side right, opposite
lateral edges, and near/far depth split. Each is a measured two-person
fixture inside the qualified safe zone. Camera visibility alone cannot prove
collision clearance, action space, viewing usability, or safe recovery.

### Identity and occlusion

The 12 scenarios cover static side-by-side tracking, lateral swaps, center
crossing, depth-order exchange, each player in front, each player crouched
behind the other, synchronized and opposing motion, and each player's exit
and re-entry. Four ordered pair classes by five placements by 12 scenarios
produce 240 cells. Twenty valid trials per cell require 4,800 identity
trials.

Every observation uses an independent per-player oracle. Record switches,
fragmentation, false transfers, missing landmarks, and wrong-track recovery.
An action that happens to score correctly does not erase an identity fault.

### Independent actions

The 14 scenarios exercise each player's left/right Dodge, Duck, and Jump;
simultaneous Jump and Duck; opposite Jump/Duck assignments; and inward and
outward opposing Dodges. Four ordered pair classes by five placements by 14
scenarios produce 280 cells and 5,600 valid trials.

Every action binds the expected player, exposure time, delivered Motion
event, and receiving game slot. Report precision and recall separately by
player, pair class, placement, and scenario. Capture arrival or inference
completion is not exposure-to-game-API latency.

### Session, ownership, freeze, and recovery

Twenty-four scenarios cover sequential and competing join attempts,
independent calibration, initial launcher ownership, both pause owners,
equal-update and earlier-completion pause races, non-owner rejection,
launcher transfer, each single-player and both-player loss, correct and wrong
silent reacquisition, timeout and deliberate recovery, explicit roster
reduction, leave/re-entry for each slot, tracker restart, camera disconnect,
and clock regression.

Four ordered pair classes by 24 scenarios produce 96 cells and 1,920 valid
cycles. Every freeze cycle records a state digest before freeze, during
freeze, and after deliberate safe recovery. Zero simulation, timer, hazard,
score, or action advancement is permitted while frozen.

### Accessible identity and owner comprehension

Eight tasks require each ordered pair to identify its own and the other
number/pattern outline, join in the assigned order, identify launcher and
pause owners, recover the full roster, and deliberately choose roster
reduction or Exit. This yields 32 cells and 640 valid trials. Color-only,
self-report-only, instructor rescue, or the other player's success cannot
qualify a task.

### Runtime soaks

Run one hour on both the console shell and obstacle sample for every ordered
pair and placement: 40 separate soaks. Record per-player pose/coverage,
actions, menu ownership, frame pacing, CPU, GPU, resident memory, power,
temperature, drops, faults, recovery, and state integrity. A shell pass does
not qualify the game adapter, and a one-player soak cannot qualify two-player
load.

## Acceptance boundary

The tracked plan freezes zero identity switches, false control transfers,
cross-player actions, unintended privileged actions, missed global freezes,
state advances while frozen, incorrect menu-owner events, wrong-track silent
recoveries, automatic recovery-overlay resumes, and invalid attempts. It
retains the existing per-player 95% trigger precision, 90% trigger recall,
and 120 ms exposure-to-correct-player game-API p95 gates.

Seventeen other numeric gates remain null: cohort size, pose FPS, coverage,
jitter, fragmentation, join/calibration time, accessible comprehension,
loss and recovery timing, pause lockout, CPU, GPU, memory, wall power,
temperature, game FPS, and frame time. They must be approved before any
participant or physical execution. No aggregate improvement may rescue a
failed pair, placement, scenario, task, runtime, or gate.

## Evidence and privacy

The repository/result envelope permits only opaque session-local trial and
player labels, bounded closed vocabularies, statistics, timestamps, and
digests. It excludes raw video, frames, images, depth, audio, names, stable
identifiers, faces, portraits, profile IDs, body measurements, calibration
vectors, identity templates, paths, free text, and network egress.

Any temporary diagnostic image requires a separate reviewed protocol,
consent/assent, authorization, restricted custody, and verified deletion.
Failed, invalid, stopped, retried, and adverse attempts remain visible.

## Honest stopping point

This document and its validator make the future campaign reviewable. They do
not authorize room access, participants, camera use, runtime mutation,
collection, qualification, product claims, or four-player work.
